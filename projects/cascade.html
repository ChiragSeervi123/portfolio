The problem

India has more than 6,000 large dams, many of them above towns that have grown up in the valley below. If one fails, a district administration has very little time to answer: how much water leaves, where it goes, when it arrives, how deep and how fast it is, and who is standing in it.

Smart India Hackathon problem statement PS 26161, from the National Technical Research Organisation (NTRO), asks for exactly this: “Dam Break Inundation Modelling Using Hydrodynamic Modelling of any River” (theme: Disaster Management, category: Software).

What it does

  • Simulates a dam break, a glacial-lake or landslide-dam outburst, a controlled gated release or a cloudburst, then plays the flood on a 3D terrain map while it is still being computed.
  • Runs the same flood through two independent solvers, a 2D shallow-water grid solver and an SPH particle solver, and maps where they disagree as model uncertainty.
  • Counts who is in the way, using OpenStreetMap settlements, hospitals, schools, bridges and roads: people exposed, arrival time per place, AIDR H1–H6 hazard classes, roads cut, indicative damage and loss of life with and without warning.
  • Checks the model against reality: import a Sentinel-1 observed flood extent, or a maximum-depth raster from Delft3D or HEC-RAS, and score the match.
  • Exports KML, zipped Shapefile, GeoJSON, ESRI ASCII rasters, an evacuation CSV sorted by arrival time, a CAP 1.2 alert in English and Hindi, and a printable one-page brief.
  • Ships with four real scenarios (Bhakra on the Sutlej, Tehri, Machchhu II 1979, Rishi Ganga 2021), and can build a new one for any dam in its catalogue.

How it works

Scroll the diagram sideways →

Everything runs in the browser, with no server and no install. A breach outflow, worked out from the dam's reservoir volume and height, feeds two hydrodynamic solvers. The grid solver solves the 2D shallow-water equations with a finite-volume method. It runs as a WebGPU shader on the graphics card or in a Web Worker, and at the start of each run both are timed on the same slice so the faster one is kept. The SPH solver models the same physics as tens of thousands of moving parcels of water. Results stream to a deck.gl map, so the flood plays out as it is worked out.

Evidence

  • ~1e-12 % Mass-balance error in the engine verification run (simulation)
  • ≤ 0.6 % Stoker wet-bed bore: position error against the exact analytical solution (simulation)
  • ≤ 9 % Ritter dry-bed dam break: front position error on 10 m cells (simulation)
  • 144 dams In the scenario builder's searchable catalogue, across 30 states

Figures as reported in the project README from its npm run verify checks. Cascade is a planning and exercise tool, not a forecast or an official warning; its depths, arrival times and losses are indicative.
