The problem

Is this price more likely to go up, go down or stay close to its mean? Chart Reader answers that from a screenshot of a price chart, for example USD/INR, Nifty 50 or Bitcoin from TradingView. It applies every topic of the course syllabus to the chart: descriptive statistics, probability and distributions, hypothesis tests and regression.

The project's one rule: every statistical computation runs in R, including reading the image. JavaScript only handles the interface and draws the charts.

Formerly FXLab (Currency Exchange Rate and Volatility Analysis Using R).

What it does

  • Takes a chart screenshot by browsing, drag-and-drop or paste: a PNG or JPEG of a candlestick, line or area chart, in a dark or light theme.
  • Reads the chart in R: OCR of the price axis and legend, a fit from pixel row to price (it also spots a log axis), and each candle's open, high, low and close from its green or red pixels.
  • Describes the returns per candle: mean, median, mode, quartiles, variance, moments, skewness and kurtosis, with a histogram and fitted normal curve.
  • Models probability: P(up), conditional probabilities and Bayes' theorem, plus binomial, Poisson and normal models compared against what was observed.
  • Runs nine hypothesis tests at α = 0.05, each shown with its formula, hypotheses, critical value, p-value and decision, then correlation and regression with robust standard errors.
  • Ends with its own hypothesis (up, down or close to the mean line, with an expected price range after 10 candles), a downloadable report, and a local library of past charts.

How it works

Scroll the diagram sideways →

One R process does all the work, and plumber serves both the API and the web page. chart_image.R decodes the screenshot, finds the background colour, reads the axis labels with Tesseract OCR and fits price against pixel row. It then treats each group of strongly green or red pixel columns as a candle: the top and bottom give the high and low, and the body gives the open and close. chart_analysis.R runs the statistics on the extracted closing prices and returns everything as JSON. The browser only draws it.

Evidence

  • 0.01–0.03 % Average error of the closing prices read from the test screenshots (synthetic prices)
  • ≤ 2 % Difference between the volatility read from the test screenshots and the true volatility
  • 9 tests Hypothesis tests run on every chart at α = 0.05
  • 4 fixtures Test screenshots covering dark, light and dense candles and a line chart

From the README's accuracy notes and the testthat suite. Prices are read from pixels, so each is accurate to about one pixel row. The outlook is a statistical description under a random-walk model, not a forecast or investment advice.
